The trap: a fake "prove you're human" check

A convincing page that looks like a normal security check or a document that "won't open". Its only real job is to get you to follow the steps without stopping to think.

A believable trick
2

A hidden instruction is copied for you

When you click the button, it quietly copies a hidden instruction onto your clipboard, not the harmless "code" you were told it would copy. You never see what was really copied.

Sleight of hand
3

You paste and press Enter yourself

The steps have you paste that hidden instruction into a small Windows tool and press Enter. Because you ran it, your computer treats it as something you meant to do, so it doesn't warn you.

You do the attacker's work
4

A hidden command runs in the background

What looked like a short "code" was actually a longer, hidden instruction. It runs out of sight, with no window you can see, so nothing looks like it went wrong.

Nothing looks wrong
5

Your computer contacts the attacker

That hidden instruction quietly connects to the attacker's website and downloads the actual harmful program onto your machine.

The harmful program takes over

Now it can steal your passwords, read your files, or lock your computer for ransom, all while acting as if it were you. This can happen in seconds.

✋ Quick check: would you do it?

Three situations. Decide for yourself before you pick.

A file a colleague shared won't open. The page tells you to press Windows + R and paste a “verification token”.

A website shows a normal “I'm not a robot” checkbox. You tick it and the page carries on as usual.

A notification from the Windows taskbar asks you to restart to finish installing updates.

🚩 The lesson: how to spot & stop ClickFix

A website asks you to press keys

No legitimate CAPTCHA or document ever needs Win+R, Ctrl+V and Enter. That is always an attack.

"Paste this to verify / fix it"

Real verification happens in the page. Being told to paste something into Windows is the tell-tale sign.

You didn't copy anything

If a page claims a "code" is ready on your clipboard that you never copied yourself, something put it there for you.

Rushed, and a strange web address

Pressure to act fast, plus a look-alike address (extra words, odd spelling). Walk away.

The padlock proves nothing

Attackers get certificates for free, so fake pages show the padlock too. It only means nobody can eavesdrop on the connection, not that the site is honest. Judge the page by what it asks you to do.

"Verification" that runs commands

Real identity checks are an emailed code, an MFA prompt on your phone, or a normal sign-in page. None of them ever ask you to run something on your computer.

A shared file needs no "fix"

Documents from colleagues or partners just open. If one needs a repair step, a plug-in or a token pasted into Windows, it isn't a document.

✅ If this happens to you: Don't press any keys. Just close the tab. If you already pasted and pressed Enter, disconnect from wi-fi/network and call the Security team or Service Desk right away. Telling someone early keeps the damage small. You will never be in trouble for reporting it.
